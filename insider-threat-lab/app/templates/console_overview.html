The problem we watch for

Criminals steal from bank customers in three ways this centre tracks: guessing staff passwords, insiders copying customer data, and Android banking malware on customers’ phones — fake screens, stolen SMS codes, apps that tap for the user. Three in four South African smartphones run Android, and Kaspersky counted 162,275 new mobile banking trojan packages in the first three months of 2026 alone. BlueWatch turns raw security events into clear incidents a person can act on.

Activity by day

How to use this

Each bar is one day. Red is attack activity (failed sign-ins, rejected API keys), orange is customer data being viewed, blue is normal sign-ins, purple is Android app checks. A tall red or orange bar is a day to investigate.

How to use this

Incidents are problems BlueWatch found that someone must look at. Open the Incidents page to assign one to a person, tag it, and close it when done.

Who viewed the most customer records

How to use this

Staff normally look up a few customers at a time. One person viewing hundreds or thousands of records can mean an insider copying data. Check the top names against their job.

Accounts with failed sign-ins

How to use this

Many failed sign-ins on one account usually means someone is guessing the password. Lock or reset the account and check where the attempts came from.
